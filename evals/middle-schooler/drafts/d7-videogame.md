# How a Battle Pass Works

A battle pass is a list of rewards you unlock by playing. It runs for a season, usually about three months, and then it resets and a new one starts.

The pass has levels, normally a hundred of them. Every level has a reward attached — a skin, an emote, a weapon charm, some currency. You move up a level by earning experience, and you earn experience by playing matches and finishing challenges.

There are two tracks. The free track gives you a reward every few levels. The paid track gives you a reward at almost every level, and it costs money to unlock. If you buy the paid track partway through the season, you immediately get everything you already passed on it.

Challenges are where most of the experience comes from. Daily challenges are small and reset every day. Weekly challenges are bigger and stack up if you miss them. Playing a match without doing any challenges earns experience too, but slowly.

Most passes include enough premium currency to buy the next season's pass. If you finish every season, you only pay once.

The reason the season ends is that the rewards go away when it does. A skin from season two is not available in season five. People who played then have it and people who started later cannot get it.

One more thing worth knowing: experience is usually capped per week on
the match side, but not on the challenge side. So grinding matches all
weekend stops paying off after a while, and the people who finish early
are the ones doing challenges rather than the ones playing the most.

That is the whole system. Play matches, finish challenges, climb levels, collect rewards, and do it before the season timer runs out.
