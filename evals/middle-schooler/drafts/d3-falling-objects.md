# Which Falls Faster?

Drop a bowling ball and a tennis ball from the same height at the same moment. Which one hits the ground first?

Most people say the bowling ball. It is heavier, so it seems like it should be pulled down harder and arrive sooner. This is one of the oldest wrong answers in science, and almost everyone gives it.

They land at the same time.

It is true that the bowling ball is pulled down harder. But the bowling ball also has more stuff in it to move, and moving more stuff takes more pull. The two effects cancel out exactly. A heavier object gets a stronger pull and needs a stronger pull, and the result is that everything speeds up at the same rate.

You can check this yourself. Take two books of very different weights and drop them side by side from shoulder height. Watch the floor, not the books. They hit together.

The reason this is hard to believe is that we live surrounded by air, and air gets in the way. A feather falls slowly, but not because it is light. It falls slowly because it is wide and floppy and has to push a lot of air out of the way as it goes. A crumpled-up ball of paper falls much faster than a flat sheet of paper, even though they weigh exactly the same amount. Same paper. Same weight. Different speed. The only thing that changed was the shape.

In 1971, an astronaut on the Moon dropped a hammer and a falcon feather at the same time. There is no air on the Moon. They landed together, and the whole thing is on video. It looks fake. It is not.

So the rule is simpler than it looks. Without air, everything falls at the same rate, no matter how heavy it is. With air, shape matters and weight still does not.
