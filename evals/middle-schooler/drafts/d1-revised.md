# How Plants Make Their Own Food

Here is something most people get wrong, including a lot of adults. Plants do not get their food from the soil.

That sounds wrong. Roots go into the dirt, so it looks like the plant is eating the dirt. But if that were true, a plant pot would slowly empty out as the plant grew. Weigh the soil in a pot, grow a plant in it for a year, and weigh the soil again. It is almost exactly the same. The plant got heavier. The soil did not get lighter.

So where did the plant come from?

Mostly from the air. Air is not nothing. It is a thin material made of gases, and one of those gases is carbon dioxide. That gas is the raw material a plant builds itself out of. The plant pulls it in through tiny holes on the underside of its leaves, too small to see.

Water comes up from the roots. That part is true. Roots do take in water, and a little bit of mineral, but not food.

Then the plant needs power to put those pieces together, and it gets that from sunlight. Sunlight is not a material. Nothing from the sun becomes part of the plant. Sunlight is the power source, like the electricity that runs a machine. The machine is not made of electricity.

So: carbon dioxide from the air, water from the roots, sunlight for power. The plant puts them together and makes a kind of sugar. That sugar is the plant's food. It is also what the plant is built out of — wood, leaves, stems, all of it, assembled from that sugar.

There is a leftover. When the plant takes apart the water, it does not need the oxygen, so it lets it go. Every bit of oxygen you have ever breathed came out of a plant as waste.

This all happens inside the leaf, in tiny green packets too small to see without a microscope. Those packets are green, and that is why leaves are green. The green stuff is what catches the sunlight.

So when you look at a tree, you are looking at something that built itself out of air and water, powered by sunlight, and threw away the oxygen you are breathing right now.
