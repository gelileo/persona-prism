# How Plants Make Their Own Food

Every living thing needs food to survive. Animals eat other organisms, but plants do something far more remarkable: they manufacture their own food out of thin air and light. This process is called photosynthesis, and it is arguably the most important chemical reaction on Earth.

Photosynthesis happens inside the chloroplast. Sunlight arrives as radiation from across the electromagnetic spectrum, though only certain wavelengths are actually absorbed — which is why leaves appear green to us. The chlorophyll molecule captures photons and uses their energy to split water into hydrogen and oxygen.

Think of the chloroplast as a tiny factory floor where the day shift converts raw inputs into usable energy currency.

The raw inputs are carbon dioxide and water. The plant pulls carbon dioxide in through tiny openings on the underside of its leaves, and draws water up from its roots through a network of narrow tubes. The outputs are glucose and oxygen. The glucose is stored and used later; the oxygen is released as waste, which is fortunate for us, since we happen to breathe it.

Energy is not stored directly as light. It is converted first into ATP, which the cell can spend wherever it is needed. This happens in two stages. The first stage depends on light and takes place in the thylakoid membranes. The second stage does not require light at all and occurs in the stroma, where carbon dioxide is fixed into organic molecules through a cycle of reactions.

So when you look at a tree, you are looking at a structure that has assembled itself almost entirely out of gas and sunlight. The mass of a tree does not come from the soil, as people often assume. It comes from the air.

This also explains why deforestation matters so much. Fewer plants means less carbon dioxide removed from the atmosphere, and less oxygen returned to it. Every leaf is running the same reaction, billions of times per second, quietly, without any noise or moving parts.

Next time you walk past a plant on a windowsill, consider what it is actually doing. It is standing perfectly still and building itself out of nothing you can see.
