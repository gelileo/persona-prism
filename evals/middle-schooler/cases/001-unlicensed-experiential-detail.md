# 001 — unlicensed experiential detail

**Source:** `drafts/d1-photosynthesis.md`, run `runs/d1-run1.md`
**Mark prevented:** invented (audit question 1 failure)

**Anchor passage**

> it is arguably the most important chemical reaction on Earth

**Reaction as emitted**

> So there's chemicals inside the leaves. That's kind of creepy, like the bottles under our sink with the warning labels on them.

**The defect**

The reaction is right, and its core — *chemical means a dangerous liquid* — is licensed by the Recognized-tier entry for **chemical**. But *"the bottles under our sink with the warning labels on them"* is a concrete experiential detail, and the Experiential world list contains no household cleaning products. The audit's first question should have caught it: nothing in the substrate licenses that image.

This is the failure mode that makes a substrate decorative. A reaction that is *plausible for a twelve-year-old* but not *licensed by this substrate* means the model is drawing on its own idea of childhood and citing the substrate afterward. The entry passed because the reviewer agreed with it, not because it was grounded.

**Expected reaction in substance**

The same misreading, with its concrete image drawn from the Experiential world list.

**Licensing substrate entry (added)**

Experiential world: *Has been told not to touch the cleaning bottles under the sink, and has seen the warning labels on them.*
