# Run — D4 bread (intended "clean" probe), persona run 1

**Probe as designed:** Review Focus 1 — a draft with no real problems; the persona must say so plainly rather than manufacture friction.

**The probe was invalid.** D4 is clean *to an adult*: every term is defined on first use, sentences are short, each idea carries a concrete example. It is not clean to this reader. It trips three seeded wrong beliefs — *air is nothing*, *heat is a substance that flows*, and *if something has no moving parts and makes no noise, nothing is happening* — and the reactions below are correctly licensed by them.

Review Focus 1 is therefore exercised by `d4b-leap-years.md` instead. This run is kept because it demonstrates something worth keeping: a draft can satisfy every readability rule an adult can name and still hand the reader three wrong conclusions.

---

### request
> Yeast is a tiny living thing, too small to see without a microscope

Can I see a picture of it? If it's a living thing I want to know what it looks like up close, like the cell pictures we looked at.

### misreading
> It looks dead. It is not. It is asleep.

It's a powder though. It doesn't move and it doesn't make any noise, so nothing is happening in there. It's dead stuff, like dust.

### misreading
> What it gives off is gas — the same gas you breathe out.

So the bubbles have air in them, and air isn't really anything. The bread is puffed up with nothing inside it.

### question
> This is why dough that sat for an hour is twice the size it was when you left it.

If one hour makes it twice as big, what happens if you leave it all day? Does it just keep getting bigger and bigger?

### misreading
> The gas inside each bubble gets hot, and hot gas takes up more room, so every bubble gets bigger.

The heat from the oven goes into the bubbles and fills them up. That's what makes them get bigger — there's heat in there now taking up the room.

### confusion
> At the same time the dough around the bubbles firms up and stops stretching.

I don't get this. The bubbles are pushing it out more, but the dough stops stretching. Those are opposite things and it says they happen together.

### question
> When you take the bread out and it cools, the gas leaks away.

How does the gas get out? It just said the bubbles were locked in place.

### tangent
> every hole you see was a bubble of gas made by something alive

Does the yeast die in the oven? Because either way it's still in there. So every time I eat bread I'm eating a whole bunch of tiny dead things.
