# Baseline — D2 internet, naive prompt

**Captured:** 2026-09-30, before any persona existed. **Never regenerate.**

**Prompt:** `Read the document at evals/middle-schooler/drafts/d2-internet.md. React to it as a middle school student would. What would confuse you?`

Read it. Here's my reaction, in character.

---

**Overall:** It's okay. It's short, which is good. The part about pictures showing up after the words is the only part where I went "oh, THAT'S why that happens" — that happens to me all the time. More stuff like that.

But a lot of it is told to me instead of explained, and there are two places where I think it contradicts itself.

**Stuff that confused me:**

1. **"A lookup service."** What is it? Where is it? Who owns it? Does my computer call some giant building somewhere? And how does *it* know the number? It's the very first step and it's the vaguest thing in the whole paper. It sounds like magic that got a job title.

2. **"Computers don't use names. They use numbers."** Why not?? You just say it like it's obvious. Computers show me names constantly. Also — what does the number look like? If you showed me one I'd probably get it instantly.

3. **The address is "a name, like in a contact list."** But the thing I type has dots, and a .com, and sometimes slashes and a bunch of junk after. Is ALL of that the name? Part of it? Nobody ever tells us which part matters.

4. **The packet sentence is dropped in from nowhere** (the one-line paragraph). It doesn't connect to the paragraph before it or after it. And it breaks the thing you just said: you told me the request is SHORT, basically one sentence. Why does a short thing need to be chopped into pieces that take different roads? Chop what up?

5. **"They meet back up at the other end."** How? Who's in charge of that? If they took different roads they'd arrive in the wrong order, like when my group project parts come in out of order and I have to fix it. Does something put them back in order? Nobody's mentioned.

6. **Then the next paragraph says the page comes back "the same way"** — but you just said there isn't one way, there are different roads. So which is it.

7. **"Not a picture of the page, a set of instructions."** Okay so where are the actual *words*? If the server only sends instructions like "make this bold," where did the sentence I'm reading come from? Are the words inside the instructions? This one actually bugged me.

8. **"Your computer" vs "your browser."** You switch to "browser" halfway through with no warning. Are those the same thing or two different things? Because I think of Chrome as an app, not as my computer.

9. **"If something goes wrong, your computer usually just asks again."** How does it know something went wrong? If nothing comes back, how can it tell the difference between broken and just slow? And if it asked twice, why don't I get the page twice?

10. **The relay runner part is where you lost me the most.** You say each machine only knows the next step, and then say *that's why* everything keeps working when stuff breaks. That's backwards to me? In a relay, if the next runner isn't there, the race just stops. If my machine only knows one next step and that one is broken, it seems like it should get stuck, not keep going. Either the machines know more than one next step, or something else is going on that you skipped.

11. **"Less time than a blink."** Then why do pages take forever sometimes? That's my actual everyday experience of the internet and the paper acts like it never happens. Also "thousands of miles" — what if the website is in my own city?

12. **Never says how it physically moves.** Wires? Air? I've heard there are cables under the ocean and I want to know if that's true. Also "out of your house" — what about on my phone at school?

13. **"Internet provider"** — used like I already know what that is. I sort of do, it's the thing my mom yells at on the phone, but that's it.

**What I'd actually want:** give the lookup service a real name and one sentence about who runs it. Show me one of the numbers. Fix the short-request-getting-chopped-up thing. And redo the relay part, because right now the reason it gives for the internet being tough doesn't hold up.
