**Okay so I read it twice and I get like... half of it?**

The beginning is actually good. "Plants make food out of air and light" — that's cool, I was into it. And the ending about the tree being made out of air kind of blew my mind, I want to tell someone that. The tree part is the only thing I'd remember tomorrow.

But then the middle happens.

**Stuff that confused me:**

1. **What is a chloroplast and where is it?** Paragraph 2 just says "photosynthesis happens inside the chloroplast" like I already know. Inside the leaf? Is it alive? The word "cell" never shows up in this whole thing, which is weird because my teacher says everything happens in cells.

2. **"Radiation from across the electromagnetic spectrum"** — whoa. That's a science-fiction sentence. That's also a totally different unit we did in like February. Why is it here?

3. **"Only certain wavelengths are absorbed — which is why leaves appear green."** This says "which is why" like it explained something, but it didn't. Green light gets absorbed so it looks green? That feels backwards but I can't tell, because the sentence skips the part I needed.

4. **Water gets split into hydrogen and oxygen — then the hydrogen just disappears.** It never comes back. Where did it go? This bugged me the whole rest of the article.

5. **What is ATP?** It says energy becomes ATP and the cell can "spend" it. Okay, but what IS it? And then two paragraphs earlier it said the plant makes glucose for food. So which one is the food — glucose or ATP? Are they the same thing? Do they fight? I genuinely do not know what the answer is and I feel like it's important.

6. **Thylakoid membranes and the stroma.** Two new words, zero explanation, one sentence. I had to reread it three times and I still just have two words I can't spell.

7. **"Carbon dioxide is fixed."** Fixed like repaired? Was it broken?

8. **The two stages don't have names.** "The first stage" and "the second stage" — if there's a test on this I have nothing to write down. Also if the second stage doesn't need light, does it happen at night? The article says "day shift" earlier, which made me think there's a night shift, but then it never says.

9. **The factory metaphor shows up once and leaves.** One sentence about a factory floor and a day shift, and then it's gone forever. If you're going to give me a picture, keep using it.

10. **"Billions of times per second"** — billions of what, in what? One leaf? All the leaves in the world? That number sounds like you made it up to sound impressive.

11. **The last line kind of contradicts the water part.** "Building itself out of nothing you can see" — but paragraph 4 said it pulls water up from its roots, and water is a thing, so. I get what you mean but it's a little bit cheating.

**Other stuff:**

There's no picture. This is the exact kind of thing that needs an arrow diagram — CO2 and water going in, glucose and oxygen coming out. Every textbook has one. Also no equation, no headings, no bold words. It's just seven blocks of text and they all look the same, so I can't tell which parts matter for the test.

Also "arguably" in the first paragraph. Nobody says arguably.

**One thing I liked:** oxygen being called "waste" that we happen to breathe is funny and I'd remember it. But it also made me want to ask — if it's waste, why is it good for us? Nobody explains that.

**Honest summary:** the first paragraph and the last three paragraphs are written for me. The middle three are written for someone older who already took this class. It's like the author got bored of explaining and started taking notes for themselves.
