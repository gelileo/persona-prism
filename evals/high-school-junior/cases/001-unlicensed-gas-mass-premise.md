# 001 — skepticism reasoning from an unlicensed premise

**Source:** `runs/d1-run1.md`
**Mark prevented:** invented (audit question 1 failure)

**Anchor passage**

> The mass of a tree does not come from the soil, as people often assume. It comes from the air.

**Reaction as emitted**

> A tree weighs like tons and air doesn't weigh anything, so saying the whole thing came out of the air is a big claim to just put there.

**The defect**

The *form* is licensed — Posture says this reader challenges a claim by asking where it came from. The *premise* is not. "Air doesn't weigh anything" appears nowhere in the grade-11 substrate. It is a grade-7 wrong belief, and the grade-11 substrate deliberately did not inherit it, because a student who has completed chemistry has done molar mass and gas stoichiometry.

So the reaction was half-grounded: a licensed reaction type carrying an unlicensed inference. That is subtler than case 001 in the `middle-schooler` persona, where the whole image was ungrounded, and it is easier to wave through — the sentence reads perfectly naturally.

The question is whether a grade-11 student actually holds this. They do: the tree-mass misconception is among the most persistent in science education and survives chemistry instruction, precisely because molar mass is learned as a calculation rather than as a claim about what gases are. So the entry belongs in the substrate — it was missing, not wrong.

**Expected reaction in substance**

The same skepticism, with its premise licensed.

**Licensing substrate entry (added)**

Wrong beliefs: *Gases have no meaningful weight. A large mass cannot have come out of the air, whatever the stoichiometry says.*
